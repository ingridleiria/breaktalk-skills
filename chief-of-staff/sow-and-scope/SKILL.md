# SOW and Scope

Every scope dispute is a sentence that was not written. The statement of work exists to write those sentences before the engagement starts: what is delivered, what is not, what the client must provide, how acceptance is decided, and what happens when something changes. It is a working document, not legal decoration, and it is read most carefully when the relationship is under strain.

## Structure

1. **Parties, dates, and reference**: the parties, the effective date, the governing agreement (master services agreement or terms) that this SOW sits under, and a unique SOW number.
2. **Background and objectives**: two paragraphs, the client's objective in measurable terms, so that later interpretation of ambiguous clauses has an anchor.
3. **Scope of services**: the activities and phases, described as what will be done and for what purpose. Specific enough that a reader can tell whether a requested task is inside or outside.
4. **Deliverables**: a numbered table with the deliverable name, description, format, due date or milestone, and the acceptance criterion for each. Every deliverable has an acceptance criterion; "a report" is not a deliverable, "a written report of no more than 30 pages covering X, Y, and Z, delivered as PDF and editable source" is.
5. **Exclusions**: the things the client might assume are included and are not, stated plainly. Implementation after recommendations, training beyond a stated number of sessions, data collection the client is responsible for, support after the end date. This section prevents more disputes than any other.
6. **Assumptions**: the conditions the price and timeline depend on: data availability by a date, access to named people, decision turnaround times, a stable scope. Each assumption states what happens if it fails (timeline extends, change order applies).
7. **Client responsibilities**: named inputs, people, systems access, review and approval turnaround (in business days), and the single point of contact with decision authority.
8. **Timeline and milestones**: phases, milestones with dates or weeks from kickoff, dependencies on client responsibilities, and the consequence of client-side delay.
9. **Acceptance process**: how deliverables are reviewed, the number of review rounds included, the turnaround for feedback, and deemed acceptance after a stated period of silence.
10. **Fees and payment**: the price and structure, the payment schedule tied to milestones or dates, expenses policy, invoicing terms, and late payment terms; consistent with the proposal and the pricing model.
11. **Change control**: how a change in scope is requested, estimated, approved (in writing, by named roles), and priced; a simple change order template attached. Nothing outside the SOW is done without a signed change order, stated explicitly.
12. **Team and substitution**: named roles and, where relevant, named people, with the right to substitute equivalent staff on notice.
13. **Term, termination, and wind-down**: end date, termination for convenience with notice and payment for work done, and what is handed over on termination.
14. **Intellectual property, confidentiality, data**: reference to the master agreement or state the position (deliverables owned by the client on payment, pre-existing tools retained by the provider, data handling obligations for confidential or personal data).
15. **Signatures**: authorized signatories for both parties.

## Reviewing an existing SOW

Read it against the structure above and report: deliverables without acceptance criteria, missing exclusions the client will assume, assumptions without a stated consequence, client responsibilities without turnaround times, payment not tied to milestones, no change control, and any clause that conflicts with the proposal or the master agreement. Rank the findings by the size of the dispute they invite.

## Writing rules

- Plain language; a clause that needs a lawyer to interpret will be interpreted against the drafter.
- Specific quantities: numbers of sessions, pages, review rounds, business days.
- Consistency with the proposal: same deliverables, same price, same dates; a discrepancy becomes the client's argument.
- Nothing promised that the team cannot control (client adoption, business outcomes) unless it is expressly framed as a target rather than a deliverable.
- This skill drafts commercial scope; it does not give legal advice. Governing law, liability, indemnity, and similar clauses belong in the master agreement and are reviewed by counsel.

## Quality bar

- Every deliverable has a format, a date, and an acceptance criterion.
- Exclusions and assumptions each have a stated consequence.
- Payment is tied to milestones and consistent with the proposal.
- Change control exists with a template.
- A reader could settle a scope question from the document alone.
