# Proposal Writer

A proposal is read by the person who wants to say yes and the person who has to justify it to someone else. It succeeds when both can find, in five minutes, the problem stated in their words, the outcome they will get, what it costs, and what happens next. Everything else is supporting material.

## Inputs to gather before drafting

- The client's situation, in their own words from the discovery conversation: the problem, its cost, what they tried, what success looks like to them, who decides and who influences.
- The offer: approach, deliverables, team, duration, price and structure (from the pricing-and-resourcing-model skill).
- Constraints: budget signals, procurement rules, competing options, timing.
- Prior materials: previous proposals to this client, the firm's standard descriptions, references and cases that fit.

If the discovery notes do not contain the client's stated problem and success criteria, the proposal is premature; draft the questions to ask first.

## Structure

1. **Cover**: client name, engagement name, date, prepared by, validity date of the proposal.
2. **Your situation** (one page): the client's problem and goals in their words, with the numbers they gave. This section proves the listening happened. Nothing about the firm here.
3. **The outcome**: what will be different at the end, stated as measurable results where possible, and how they will be measured. Two to four outcomes, not a list of activities.
4. **Approach**: the phases, what happens in each, what the client's team does, and what they receive at the end of each. Written for the reader who has to explain it internally.
5. **Deliverables**: a precise list, each with a one-line description, so scope disputes have a reference. Explicit exclusions where the client might assume more (link to sow-and-scope for the formal version).
6. **Team**: who does the work, with one paragraph each on the specific relevance of their experience to this engagement. Not full bios.
7. **Timeline**: phases against calendar dates or weeks from kickoff, with milestones and the client-side inputs required to hold them.
8. **Investment**: the price, the structure (fixed, phased, retainer, options), payment schedule, and what is included and excluded. At most two options. Written plainly; no hedging language around price.
9. **Why us** (short): two or three specific reasons tied to this problem, with one relevant case each, results quantified. This section comes late because the client cares about their problem first.
10. **Terms**: the key commercial terms (validity, payment terms, IP, confidentiality, cancellation) in a short section or a reference to the standard agreement.
11. **Next step**: the specific action, the date, and who takes it. "Countersign by the 20th and we kick off the week of the 27th."

Appendix: detailed bios, case studies, methodology descriptions, references.

## Writing rules

- The client's name and vocabulary throughout; the firm's internal method names explained or replaced.
- Outcomes before activities. A phase is described by what it produces.
- Numbers wherever a claim is made: results in cases, durations, prices.
- No superlatives about the firm. No "world-class", "unique", "best-in-class", "cutting-edge".
- Short sentences. Executives read proposals on phones.
- The price appears where the reader looks for it, in a section with a heading, not buried in a paragraph.

## RFP responses

When responding to a formal RFP: build the compliance matrix first (every requirement, where it is answered), follow the requested structure exactly even when it is poor, answer every question directly before elaborating, and keep the "your situation" content for the executive summary. Non-compliance on format is the most common reason a good response is disqualified.

## Delivery

Produce the document with the environment's document skill, branded per the firm's brand skill if one exists (or the tailored-client-deck rules for co-branding when the client's logo is used). Deliver with a cover email using the outreach-email skill's post-call follow-up shape.

## Quality bar

- Section 2 could only have been written for this client.
- Each deliverable is precise enough to settle a scope dispute.
- The price and the next step are findable in under a minute.
- No claim about the firm lacks a number or a case.
