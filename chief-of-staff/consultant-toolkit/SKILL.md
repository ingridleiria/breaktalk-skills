# Consultant Toolkit

The job of a generalist consultant, and of a Chief of Staff doing consultant work, is to take something complicated and messy, break it down quickly, and get to a sensible answer that someone else can act on. This skill enforces that discipline.

## The core sequence

Every piece of work follows the same five steps. Do not skip ahead to the answer.

1. **Define the question.** One sentence, decision-shaped. "Should we enter market X by Q3" is a question. "Market X overview" is not. If the user's request is not decision-shaped, propose the decision-shaped version and confirm before proceeding.
2. **Structure the problem.** Break the question into 3 to 5 sub-questions that are mutually exclusive and collectively exhaustive. Each sub-question must be answerable with evidence. Write them down before gathering anything.
3. **Gather evidence per sub-question.** Numbers first. Every claim gets a magnitude and a source. "The market is growing" is banned; "the market grew 12% in 2025 (source)" is the standard. When a number is unavailable, say so and state what it would take to get it. Never invent figures.
4. **Form the answer.** State the recommendation in the first sentence, then support it. The reader should be able to stop after paragraph one and know what to do.
5. **Stress-test.** Before delivering, write the strongest objection a skeptical executive would raise and answer it inside the document. If the objection survives, change the recommendation.

## Output formats

Pick by audience, not by habit:

- **Decision memo** (default): recommendation first, then context, options considered, evidence, risks, and the explicit ask (approve, fund, decide by date). One to two pages.
- **Options comparison**: a table with options as columns and decision criteria as rows, each cell containing evidence rather than adjectives, followed by a recommendation paragraph. Never end on "it depends".
- **Executive summary**: five sentences maximum. Situation, complication, answer, evidence, ask.
- **Board or investor material**: headline-style titles that state the finding ("Churn concentrated in accounts under $10k"), one idea per page, numbers on every page.

## Quality bar

- Answer first. No document opens with background.
- Quantify or flag. An unquantified claim is either given a number, given a source, or explicitly marked as an assumption.
- Options are real. If only one option is presented, the analysis was not done. Include the do-nothing baseline.
- Risks are specific: what could go wrong, how likely, what it would cost, what the mitigation is. "Execution risk" alone is filler.
- The ask is explicit. Every document ends with what the reader is being asked to do and by when.
- No consulting vocabulary without content: alignment, synergies, holistic, best-in-class, unlock, drive impact are all banned unless attached to a number or a mechanism.

## Working with the user

Ask for the three things that change the answer before drafting: the decision deadline, the audience, and any constraint already fixed (budget cap, headcount freeze, a decision the CEO has effectively already made). If a constraint makes the analysis theater, say so plainly.
