# Strategic Plan and Action Plan

Most strategy documents fail at the join. The strategy half is a set of ambitions nobody disagrees with, the plan half is a list of activities already happening, and nothing connects the two. The test applied here is simple: could someone who was not in the room execute this on Monday, and would they know what to stop doing.

Sits alongside `okr-planning`, which turns the plan into measured objectives, and `operating-cadence-design`, which builds the rhythm that keeps it alive.

## Part one: the strategy

**The baseline, honestly.** Where the business actually is: the numbers, the trend, what is working and what has been quietly failing. Written before any ambition is set, and written so that someone inside the business would recognise it as true. A plan built on a flattering baseline fails at the first review, and everyone will have known.

**The destination.** One sentence, with a number and a date. Not three, not a set of themes. If the leadership team cannot agree on a single destination, that disagreement is the real work and it comes before the plan.

**What has to be true.** Between here and there sit three to five conditions. These are the bets. Each one is stated as a claim that could be false, with the evidence for believing it and the evidence that would overturn it. A condition nobody could disagree with is not a bet, it is a platitude, and it is taking a slot from a real one.

**What we will not do.** Named, specifically, including things currently being done. This is the section that makes the document a strategy rather than a wish list, and the section most often left out. A plan with no exclusions has not made a choice and will be executed at half strength everywhere.

**The risks that would break it.** Two or three, each with the early signal that it is happening, so it is noticed while there is still time.

## Part two: the action plan

One workstream per bet. Anything that does not belong to a bet either goes in the run-the-business list or gets cut, and both are visible decisions rather than omissions.

For each workstream:

| Field | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Owner | One named person, never a team or two people |
| Outcome | What is different when this is done, in a measurable form |
| Milestones | Verifiable events with dates, not percentages of completion |
| First step | Something that starts within two weeks |
| Dependencies | What it needs from elsewhere, and who owes it |
| Resources | People and money, stated, including where they come from |
| Kill criteria | What would have to be observed for this to be stopped |

Milestones describe events. "Pricing model approved by the leadership team" is a milestone; "pricing work 60 percent complete" is not, because nobody can dispute it and therefore nobody can act on it.

The kill criteria are the part that gets skipped and the part that matters most. A bet with no stopping condition never stops, it just gets quieter, and it keeps consuming people who could be somewhere else. Write the condition while everyone is still optimistic, because it cannot be written honestly later.

## Part three: keeping it alive

A plan that is not reviewed is a document. Set the review before the plan is finished: who attends, how often, and what is looked at. Monthly on milestones and dependencies, quarterly on whether the bets still look right.

The quarterly review asks three questions and writes down the answers. Is the destination still the right one. Which bets have been proved or disproved by what we learned. What are we stopping. A review that never stops anything is a status meeting.

## Common failures

A strategy that is a list of everything the company does, reordered. Objectives with no owner, or with a team as owner. Milestones as percentages. An action plan that starts in eight weeks, which means it starts never. Dependencies noted but not owed by anyone. No exclusions. No kill criteria. A destination that changes at the first review, which usually means it was never agreed.

## Quality bar

- The baseline is one a sceptical insider would accept as accurate.
- One destination, with a number and a date.
- Three to five bets, each falsifiable, each with the evidence that would overturn it.
- An explicit list of what will not be done, including things currently being done.
- Every workstream has one named owner, dated event milestones, a first step inside two weeks, and stated kill criteria.
- The review cadence exists and is scheduled before the plan is circulated.
