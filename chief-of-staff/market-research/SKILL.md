# Market Research

Market research exists to change a decision. Before gathering anything, write down the decision the research feeds and what finding would change it. Research that cannot change the decision is decoration.

## Method

### 1. Frame

State the decision, the deadline, and the two or three questions that actually matter. Confirm with the user. A typical frame: "Should we enter segment X next year? Depends on: is the segment large enough (> $Ym), is it growing, and can we win against the incumbents."

### 2. Size the market honestly

Build the size two ways and reconcile them:

- **Top-down**: start from a published market figure, cut it down to the reachable slice with explicit filters (geography, segment, price point). Name the source and year of the anchor figure.
- **Bottom-up**: number of potential customers times realistic annual spend per customer. State where each input comes from.

If the two methods disagree by more than 2x, say so and explain which you trust and why. A single confident number with no method shown is the mark of research that will not survive one board question. Always distinguish TAM (everyone who could buy), SAM (the slice the product can serve today), and SOM (what could realistically be won in 2 to 3 years), and state the assumptions that separate them.

### 3. Map the competition by evidence, not by logo wall

For each relevant competitor: what they sell, to whom, at what price (actual pricing where public), how they distribute, funding or revenue where known, and their most recent strategic move with a date. Sources attached to every row. Then answer the only competitive question that matters: on what basis would a customer choose us instead, and is that basis durable?

Include the non-obvious competitors: the status quo (spreadsheets, doing nothing), internal builds, and adjacent players one move away from the market.

### 4. Verify before it enters the document

- Every number carries a source and a year in the same sentence or the same table row.
- Prefer primary sources: company filings, pricing pages, regulator data, national statistics, earnings calls. Market-report press releases are a last resort and are labeled as such.
- Two independent sources for any number that carries the recommendation. If only one exists, mark the number as single-sourced.
- Dates matter. A 2021 market size in a 2026 memo is a different claim; either update it or flag it.
- Never fabricate a figure, a company detail, or a source. A gap stated plainly ("no public revenue figures; closest proxy is headcount growth of 40% in a year, per LinkedIn") is more useful than a guess.

### 5. Deliver as a decision input

Default output is a short memo: the answer to the framing question first, the market size with method shown, the competitive read, the two or three risks that could invalidate the answer, and what to watch next. Tables for competitor data, prose for the argument. End on the finding, not on "further research is recommended".

## Quality bar

- Someone who disagrees with the conclusion should still trust every number in the document.
- Each section survives the question "so what?": if a fact does not bear on the decision, cut it.
- Growth claims name the period and the base. "Growing fast" is banned.
- The document distinguishes what is known, what is estimated, and what is assumed, and never lets the three blur.
