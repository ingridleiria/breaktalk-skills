# Board Deck

A board deck has two jobs and only two: tell the board the truth about the company's condition, and get the decisions the company needs from them. Decks that perform for the board instead of informing it are recognized by experienced directors within three slides, and they cost the CEO credibility that is hard to recover.

## Before building

Establish with the user:

- The board's composition: investors, independents, founders, observers. Each reads the deck for different things (investors for the metrics against plan, independents for risk and governance, founders for strategy).
- What the board decided or asked for last time. Every open board request gets a status in this deck.
- The decisions needed this meeting: approvals, appointments, budget, financing, major contracts, policy. If none, say "no decisions requested" explicitly.
- The cadence and format: pre-read sent days ahead with a shorter live deck, or one document. The default that works: a pre-read of 15 to 25 pages sent five days before, and a live discussion built on ten slides at most.
- Whether there are sensitive matters (personnel, litigation, M&A) that belong in an executive session or a separate confidential note rather than the main pack.

## Pre-read structure

1. **CEO letter** (one page, prose, written in the CEO's voice): the quarter in five paragraphs. What happened, what went well, what did not and why, what management is doing about it, and what the board is being asked. Honest about misses; directors forgive misses and do not forgive surprises.
2. **Dashboard** (one page): the ten to fifteen metrics that define the business, each with actual, plan, prior period, and variance, colored only by variance (on plan, within tolerance, off plan). Same metrics every meeting, in the same order, so directors build pattern recognition.
3. **Financials**: P&L against budget, cash and runway, balance sheet summary, and the forecast for the year with the assumptions that changed since the last board meeting. Variances over a threshold (state it) get a one-line explanation each.
4. **Performance by area**: one page per function or business line (commercial, product, operations, people), each with headline, key numbers, what changed, and risks. Headlines are findings ("Enterprise pipeline doubled; SMB churn rose to 4%"), not labels.
5. **Strategic topics**: one to three items where management wants the board's thinking, each framed as a question with the options and management's recommendation. These are where board time is best spent.
6. **Decisions requested**: each on its own page with the decision stated in one sentence, the rationale, the alternatives considered, the risk, and the exact resolution language.
7. **People**: hires, departures, organization changes, compensation matters (or a pointer to the executive session).
8. **Risks and compliance**: the top risks with movement since last meeting, legal and regulatory matters, and any incident.
9. **Follow-ups from last meeting**: each board request with its status.
10. **Appendix**: detailed financials, pipeline detail, product roadmap, KPI definitions.

## Live deck

Ten slides maximum, built from the pre-read: the CEO headline, the dashboard, the two or three biggest variances, the strategic questions, and the decisions. Assume the pre-read was read; never present it again. Time allocation on the agenda: at least half the meeting on strategic topics and decisions, not on reporting.

## Writing and design rules

- Every slide title is a sentence stating the finding.
- Numbers reconcile across the deck: the same revenue figure on every page, to the same rounding.
- Definitions are stable meeting to meeting; when a definition changes, the change is flagged and the prior period restated.
- Charts show trend over time, not single points, with the plan line visible. One message per chart, legend outside the plot area, no rainbow palettes.
- Prose for the CEO letter and the strategic topics; tables and charts for the numbers; bullets only for lists of decisions and follow-ups.
- Apply the company's brand skill if one exists; otherwise a restrained palette and a single sans-serif family. When a file is required, follow the environment's presentation or document skill for generation mechanics.

## Quality bar

- A director reading only the CEO letter and the dashboard knows the company's condition and what is being asked.
- Every miss against plan is stated by management before a director can find it.
- Every decision requested has resolution language ready to be minuted.
- Every request from the previous meeting has a status.
- Nothing in the main pack belongs in executive session.
