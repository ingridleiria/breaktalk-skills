# Fundraise Readiness

Rounds are rarely lost on the commercial story. They stall on a cap table nobody can reconcile, a metric defined differently in three documents, contracts nobody can find, and a founder answering a diligence question differently on Tuesday than on Friday. All of that is preventable, and all of it takes longer to fix under time pressure than before starting.

Start eight to twelve weeks before going out. `pitch-deck` builds the argument, and `financial-model-builder` builds the model this pack reports against.

## The metrics pack

Build it once, define every metric in writing, and use that definition everywhere: the deck, the data room, the model, the update, and the answer given verbally. Inconsistency here does more damage than a weak number, because it makes a diligence team wonder what else is inconsistent.

For each metric: the definition, the source system, who owns it, the history at monthly granularity, and any change in how it has been calculated, disclosed rather than smoothed. A metric whose definition changed midway through the history is disclosed as such; discovered later, it is treated as something worse than a definition change.

Expect to be asked to reconcile the metrics to the accounts. If they cannot be reconciled, find out now.

## The data room

Structured so a diligence team can find things without asking, because every question they have to ask is a delay and a small deposit of doubt.

Company and corporate: incorporation documents, the cap table with every instrument, board and shareholder consents, minutes, and the option plan with grants. Financials: statements by year and month, the model, revenue by customer and by month, and the reconciliation between the metrics and the accounts. Commercial: the customer list with contract value and dates, standard and non-standard contracts, churn history with reasons, and the pipeline. People: the org chart, the employee list with compensation and equity, employment agreements, contractor agreements, and confirmation that intellectual property is assigned to the company. Legal: intellectual property registrations, licences, any litigation or dispute, insurance, and any regulatory matter. Product and technology: architecture, security posture, third-party dependencies, and any data protection documentation.

Index it. Version it. Restrict it, and keep an access log.

## Corporate hygiene, which is where rounds actually stall

Work through these before anything else, because each takes weeks to fix and none can be fixed under a signed term sheet.

The cap table reconciles to the issued instruments and to the option plan, including anything promised verbally and never papered. Every founder and employee has assigned intellectual property to the company in writing, and so has every contractor and agency. Board and shareholder consents exist for every issuance. Any advisor or early contributor with a claim, however informal, is documented and settled. Customer contracts have been checked for change of control and assignment clauses. Nothing material is held personally by a founder.

## One narrative

The story in the deck, the numbers in the model, the metrics in the pack, and what is said in the room are the same story. Write the two or three sentences that are the argument, and check every document against them.

Then write down the weaknesses, honestly, and decide the answer for each one in advance. Every company has three or four. Concentration, churn in a cohort, a flat quarter, a departure, a dependency. A weakness named by the company reads as self-awareness. The same weakness discovered in diligence reads as concealment, and it repriced the round.

## Rehearse the diligence

Ask the questions that will be asked, and answer them out loud until the answer is stable and the same from everyone.

What actually drives growth and is it repeatable. What happens to a cohort over twenty-four months. Why did those customers leave. What is the real sales cycle and win rate. Which revenue is contracted and which is not. How dependent is this on one channel, one customer, or one person. What breaks first if you triple. Why has this team not shipped the thing announced last year. What did the last round's plan say, and how did the outcome compare.

That last one is asked more often than founders expect, and the previous plan is in the previous data room.

## The process

Set the calendar: preparation, a partner meeting sequence with the firms grouped so the process runs in parallel rather than sequentially, diligence, and signing. Keep a tracker of who has what and who owes what. Assign one person to own the data room and one to own the answers, and route everything through them so no two people answer the same question differently.

## Quality bar

- Every metric has one written definition used in every document and every conversation.
- The metrics reconcile to the accounts, and any historical change of definition is disclosed.
- The cap table reconciles, and intellectual property assignment is documented for everyone who touched the product.
- The data room is indexed, versioned, access controlled, and complete before the first meeting.
- The three or four weaknesses are named by the company first, each with a prepared answer.
- The diligence questions have been rehearsed until the answers match across the founding team.
