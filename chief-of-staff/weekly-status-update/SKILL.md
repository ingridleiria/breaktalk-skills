# Weekly Status Update

A status update has one reader and one purpose: the person deciding where to intervene, deciding faster. Activity lists do not help that reader. Exceptions do. This skill produces updates that are honest about condition, short enough to be read, and identical in shape week after week.

## Two layers

### Layer one: the workstream update (written by each owner)

Three lines per workstream, no more:

- **Done**: what shipped or completed this week, stated as outcomes ("contract signed", "v2 live to all users"), not effort ("worked on the contract").
- **Next**: what will be done by next week, specific enough to check.
- **Blocked or at risk**: the blocker, who can remove it, and since when; or the risk to a date or metric with the recovery plan. If nothing, write "none", because an empty line reads as forgotten.

Plus a status word: on track, at risk, or blocked, chosen honestly. "On track" repeated for five weeks before a slip means the earlier updates were false, and the reader learns to discount everything.

### Layer two: the leadership rollup (written by the Chief of Staff)

1. **Headline** (two sentences): the week's condition across the portfolio and the one thing the leader must act on.
2. **Needs a decision or intervention**: each item with the decision needed, from whom, by when, and the cost of waiting. This section is the reason the update exists. If empty, say "nothing this week".
3. **At risk or blocked**: the workstreams not on track, one line each, with the recovery plan or the unblock needed.
4. **Shipped this week**: the completed outcomes, one line each, with numbers where they exist.
5. **Metrics**: the five to eight numbers tracked weekly, same order every week, with movement since last week.
6. **Coming next week**: the milestones due, so the reader knows what to expect.
7. **Full workstream table** (appendix): every workstream's three lines and status, for the reader who wants detail.

Under 400 words above the appendix. If the rollup is longer, it is reporting activity.

## Consolidation method

When raw updates arrive from several people:

1. Extract the three lines and the status from each, rewriting activity as outcomes where the owner wrote activity.
2. Identify exceptions: anything at risk, blocked, slipped, or needing a decision. These move up to sections 2 and 3.
3. Detect what the owners did not say: a workstream with no update, a milestone due this week that was not mentioned, a "next" that repeats last week's "next". Flag each; silence is a signal.
4. Check the numbers against the source of truth, not against last week's update.
5. Write the headline last, after the exceptions are visible.

## Writing rules

- Verbs in the past tense for done, future for next, present for blocked.
- Names for owners, dates for everything due. No "soon", "shortly", "in the coming weeks".
- No adjectives about progress ("great week", "solid momentum"). The outcomes carry the tone.
- Consistent order of workstreams and metrics every week; the reader should be able to find any item by position.
- A risk stated one week and resolved the next is closed explicitly, so the reader is not left tracking it.

## Quality bar

- The leader can act on the update after reading the headline and section 2.
- Every non-green status has a recovery plan or an unblock request attached.
- Every workstream appears, including the ones with no update, marked as such.
- The update is the same shape as last week's.
