# Event and Offsite Planning

An offsite costs a leadership team's full attention for one to three days plus travel; it is the most expensive meeting the company holds. Most produce good conversation and no decisions. This skill designs the event backwards from the decisions and outputs it must produce, then handles the logistics so that nothing but the content needs attention on the day.

## Step 1: The outcomes

Before venues or dates, write:

- The two to four outcomes the event must produce, as artifacts: "an agreed set of annual priorities with owners", "a decision on the pricing change", "a leadership team that has met its three new members in person". If an outcome cannot be written as something that exists after the event, it is a mood, and moods are fine as secondary goals but not as the reason to fly people in.
- The audience and the number, with the constraints (time zones, travel, accessibility, budget, dates blocked).
- The decision-maker for the agenda (usually the CEO) and the owner of the event (usually the Chief of Staff).

## Step 2: The agenda, backwards

Design each session from its output:

- **Session output**: the decision, the document, the list, or the shared understanding the session must produce.
- **Inputs**: the pre-read or data the session needs, and who prepares it by when. Pre-work is the difference between a session that decides and one that discusses; sessions that require reading start with the reading done.
- **Format**: presentation and debate, small groups then plenary, individual writing then discussion, a structured decision process. Vary formats; three consecutive presentations lose the room.
- **Facilitation**: who runs it, with a written flow and timings, and who captures decisions and actions (see meeting-to-decisions).
- **Timing**: with breaks every ninety minutes, the hardest decision before lunch on the first day, and a closing session that reads back every decision and action.

Build the day plan with buffers; agendas that fill every minute produce a rushed close, and the close is where the value is captured. Social time is scheduled, not left over, and placed where it serves the outcomes (a dinner after the hardest session, not before it).

## Step 3: Logistics and budget

A tracker with owners and dates: venue (room layout matching the formats, natural light, breakout space, reliable connectivity, quiet), dates and travel, accommodation, food that accounts for dietary needs, equipment (screens, whiteboards, materials, printing), transport, a budget with a contingency of 10 to 15%, contracts and cancellation terms, communication to participants (a joiner pack with the agenda, pre-work, logistics, and the outcomes), and an on-site run sheet for the day with times and responsibilities.

For client events and conference presences, add: the objective in pipeline terms (meetings booked, leads with follow-up), the target list of attendees or visitors, invitations and reminders, the materials (using tailored-client-deck and the brand skill), staffing rota, the capture process for conversations, and the follow-up sequence sent within 48 hours.

## Step 4: Risk and contingency

The five things most likely to go wrong (a key participant cancels, connectivity fails, a session overruns, the room is wrong, a difficult topic derails the day) with a plan for each. A remote-participation fallback prepared even if nobody is expected to need it.

## Step 5: Capture and follow-up

- During the event, decisions and actions captured in real time and displayed; the closing session reads them back and confirms owners and dates.
- Within 24 hours: the record circulated (meeting-to-decisions format), the decision log updated, the actions loaded into the tracker.
- Within one week: a short participant survey on outcomes achieved and format, and the owner's own retrospective written for the next event.
- At 30 days: a check on the actions, reported in the weekly status. An offsite whose actions are not tracked was a retreat.

## Deliverables

The outcomes statement, the agenda with session designs and pre-work assignments, the logistics tracker and budget, the joiner pack, the run sheet, the facilitation notes, the capture template, and the follow-up plan.

## Quality bar

- Every session has a named output and a pre-read owner.
- The closing session is protected and reads back every decision.
- The budget includes contingency and the contracts have known cancellation terms.
- Decisions are circulated within 24 hours and checked at 30 days.
