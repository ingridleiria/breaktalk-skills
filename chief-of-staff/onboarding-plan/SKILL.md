# Onboarding Plan

The first ninety days decide whether a hire works, and they are mostly decided by whether anyone planned them. A written plan turns "settle in" into milestones, names the people the new hire must meet, and gives both sides a shared definition of success at each checkpoint. It also protects the hire from the most common failure: being busy without being useful.

## Inputs

- The scorecard for the role (from hiring-scorecard-and-interview-kit) or, failing that, the outcomes expected at twelve months.
- The organization's context: who the role works with, which systems it uses, what documents define how the company runs, the current priorities and the fires.
- Constraints: remote or on-site, time zones, the manager's availability, existing commitments the hire inherits.

## The plan structure

Written as one document, shared with the hire on day one, updated at each checkpoint.

**Before day one**: accounts and access created, equipment shipped, the plan itself sent, a welcome note from the manager, the first week's calendar populated with introductions, and a buddy assigned. A hire who spends the first week requesting access has learned that the company is disorganized.

**Days 1 to 30: learn.** Goal: understand the business, the people, and the role's current state.

- People: a list of fifteen to twenty-five people to meet, with why each matters and one question to ask each. Includes peers, the manager's peers, key people in adjacent teams, and, for senior roles, board members or key clients.
- Systems and documents: the tools, the strategy documents, the operating cadence, the metrics, the last three board packs or equivalents, the decision log.
- Observation: sit in on the recurring meetings, read the recent history, shadow the work the role will own.
- Early deliverable: by day 30, a written read-back to the manager: what the hire understands the business and the role to be, what surprised them, what they think the priorities are. This surfaces misunderstandings early and is the first evidence of judgment.
- Milestone review at day 30.

**Days 31 to 60: contribute.** Goal: own something visible and small.

- The hire takes over the first responsibilities from the scorecard, with the previous owner available.
- One deliverable with a real audience: a process improved, an analysis delivered, a meeting run.
- Relationships deepened with the five people the role depends on most.
- Feedback collected from those people by the manager and shared.
- Milestone review at day 60, against outcomes agreed on day 30.

**Days 61 to 90: own.** Goal: full ownership of the role's core, and a plan for the next quarter.

- All scorecard responsibilities transferred.
- The hire's own 90-day assessment: what is working, what is not, what they would change, and their plan for the next quarter with outcomes.
- Milestone review at day 90: a written assessment against the scorecard, and either a confirmation, an adjusted plan, or a hard conversation. Silence at day 90 is a failure of the manager.

## Check-in cadence

- Daily short check-in in week one, then twice weekly through day 30, then weekly. Each check-in has a fixed shape: what was learned or done, what is unclear, what is blocked, what is next.
- The buddy meets the hire weekly for the informal questions the hire will not ask the manager.

## The executive and Chief of Staff variant

For senior roles, add: a listening tour with a structured question set across the organization, a written diagnosis at day 30 delivered to the CEO, a first visible decision or change by day 60 chosen for low risk and high visibility, and a first-quarter plan presented to the leadership team by day 90. For a Chief of Staff, the day-30 deliverable is the operating cadence diagnosis (see operating-cadence-design), because it maps the company while introducing the hire to everyone.

## When the user is the new hire

Build the same plan from the hire's side: the questions to ask in each introduction, the documents to request, the read-back to write, and the early win to choose. Advise against proposing changes before the day-30 read-back has been received.

## Deliverables

The plan document; the introduction list with reasons and questions; the access and equipment checklist; the check-in template; the day-30, 60, and 90 review templates with the scorecard outcomes pre-filled.

## Quality bar

- Access and equipment are ready before day one.
- Every phase has a deliverable with a real audience.
- The 30, 60, and 90 reviews are dated and have templates.
- The plan names people, documents, and systems, not categories.
