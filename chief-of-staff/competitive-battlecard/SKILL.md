# Competitive Battlecard

A battlecard is read thirty seconds before a call or during it. It has to be true, current, short, and useful under pressure. Battlecards built from the competitor's marketing and the sales team's folklore fail on the first informed prospect; battlecards built from evidence and win/loss data hold.

## Evidence gathering

Before writing, collect and date each source (external-insights method):

- **Competitor's own material**: positioning statements, pricing pages, packaging tiers, case studies, documentation, release notes, job postings (which reveal what they are building), investor materials for public companies.
- **Third-party**: review sites (read the negative reviews for patterns, discount the extremes), analyst coverage, press, community forums where their users talk.
- **Internal win/loss**: from the CRM and from interviews with the last ten to fifteen deals where this competitor was present; what the prospect said, why they chose either side, what price and terms were seen. This is the most valuable source and the most often skipped.
- **Product experience**: a trial or demo of the competitor, where legitimately available, scripted against the organization's own scenarios.

Never fabricate a competitor claim, price, or weakness. An unverified point is labeled as such or omitted; a false statement about a competitor made to a prospect is both a credibility and a legal risk.

## The battlecard, one page

1. **Snapshot**: who they are (founded, size, funding, geography, target customer), and their positioning in one sentence as they state it.
2. **Where they are strong**: honestly, three to five points, because the prospect already knows them and a battlecard that denies them loses credibility. Includes where they genuinely beat the organization.
3. **Where they are weak**: three to five verified points with the evidence source (review patterns, documentation gaps, architectural limits, pricing model consequences, support model, roadmap gaps visible in job postings).
4. **How we win**: the two or three differentiators that matter to the buyer, each stated as a customer outcome with proof (a case, a number, a demo moment).
5. **Where we lose**: the deal patterns where the competitor wins, and the honest reason, so salespeople qualify out early or bring in the right help.
6. **Objections they plant and the answers**: the claims the competitor makes about the organization in deals, each with a short, factual response and the proof point to show.
7. **Questions to ask the prospect**: five to eight discovery questions that surface the competitor's weaknesses without naming them ("How important is it that the data stays in-region?", "Who will administer the system day to day?").
8. **Traps to set**: the criteria to plant early in an evaluation that the competitor struggles to meet, stated ethically, based on real requirements the prospect is likely to have.
9. **Pricing and packaging**: their tiers, list prices where public, typical discounting seen in deals, contract terms, and how to frame the comparison on total cost.
10. **Recent moves**: the last three to five dated events (releases, funding, leadership changes, outages, acquisitions) and what they signal.
11. **Sources and last updated**: the date and the person responsible.

## Comparison matrix (appendix)

Capability rows against both offerings, scored with evidence, only for capabilities that matter to the buyer. A matrix where the organization wins every row is not believed; show the rows lost.

## Maintenance

Battlecards decay in a quarter. Assign an owner, set a review cadence, and update after every notable competitor event and every quarter's win/loss review. Version and date visibly; a salesperson must know how old the information is.

## Use in the field

Pair with the outreach-email and tailored-client-deck skills for materials that use the differentiators; pair with external-insights for a deal-specific refresh when the competitor is in a live opportunity.

## Quality bar

- Every weakness claimed has a verifiable source.
- Strengths of the competitor are acknowledged.
- Win/loss data from real deals informed sections 4, 5, and 6.
- The card fits one page and can be used during a call.
- It is dated and owned.
