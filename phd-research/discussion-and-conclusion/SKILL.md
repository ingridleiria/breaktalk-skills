# Discussion and Conclusion

The results section says what was found. The discussion says what it means, how much to believe it, and what follows from it, in that order and at no greater strength than the design allows. The conclusion then closes on the finding. Neither introduces anything new.

## The discussion

Write in prose, in this order:

1. **The finding at the level of the question.** One paragraph translating the headline estimate back into the terms of the research question: not "the coefficient on treatment is 0.12", but "student financing raised learning outcomes by about a tenth of a standard deviation, roughly the gap between..." Give the reader a comparison they can picture (a percentile move, a share of the yearly gain, a monetary equivalent).
2. **The mechanism.** Why the effect exists, in the terms of the theoretical framework, with the paper's own heterogeneity or mechanism evidence cited by exhibit. Where the mechanism is inferred rather than tested, say so.
3. **Comparison with prior evidence, by number.** "Our estimate of 0.12 SD is larger than the 0.05 SD reported by [verified citation] in a comparable setting and close to the 0.10 SD in [verified citation]." Then the reason for the difference: setting, population, design, measurement. "Consistent with the literature" without numbers is not a comparison. Citations follow the literature-verification standard.
4. **Boundary conditions.** For whom, where, and when the finding holds, from the heterogeneity results, and where it likely does not, from theory. The external validity claim stated explicitly.
5. **Limitations.** Each stated plainly with the direction of the likely bias, and with the evidence that bounds it where such evidence exists. No limitation is followed by a sentence explaining why it does not matter; if it does not matter, it is not a limitation. Typical: identification threats conceded in identification-defense, measurement error in the outcome, sample coverage, attrition, the local nature of the estimand.
6. **Implications.** Policy or practice implications sized to the evidence: one implication the design clearly supports beats three it might. State the population the implication applies to and the conditions under which it would fail. Cost-effectiveness comparisons where the numbers exist.

## The conclusion

Short, one to three paragraphs, and it does three things only: restates the question and the answer with the main number, states the contribution in one or two sentences matching the introduction's move five exactly, and ends on the finding. Future research directions, if the field expects them, are one sentence naming the specific question the paper could not answer and what data would answer it; not a list.

## Field conventions

- **Economics**: discussion often folded into results and a short conclusion; magnitudes and comparisons by number are expected; policy implications brief.
- **Education**: a distinct discussion section with explicit implications for practice and policy, limitations as a labeled subsection, and a conclusion.
- **Management**: theoretical contributions stated first, then managerial implications, then limitations and future research, each often with a subheading.

Ask which venue or field, then follow its convention.

## The thesis chapter variant

A chapter conclusion also closes the chapter's place in the thesis: what this chapter established that the next chapter builds on, stated in one paragraph, and the link to the thesis question. The final chapter of the thesis synthesizes across chapters by number, states the thesis-level contribution, and ends on the thesis finding.

## Writing rules

- Numbers in the discussion are identical to the results tables.
- Causal language calibrated to the design, no upgrade in the discussion.
- No summary of the paper's structure ("this paper first presented...").
- No uplift ending ("as AI transforms education, understanding these effects becomes ever more..."). The last sentence is a finding.
- No new results, new citations that change the argument, or new claims about mechanisms not evidenced.

## Diagnosing a weak draft

Mark each paragraph by its function from the list above. Common failures: the discussion restates the results table row by row; comparisons are qualitative; limitations are each followed by reassurance; implications outrun the evidence; the conclusion introduces a new idea. Show the diagnosis, then rewrite.

## Quality bar

- The finding is restated in interpretable terms with a comparison the reader can picture.
- Prior estimates are compared by number with verified citations.
- Every limitation carries the direction of bias and no reassurance clause.
- Implications match the estimand and population.
- The final sentence is a result.
