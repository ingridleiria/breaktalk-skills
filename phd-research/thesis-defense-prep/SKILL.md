# Thesis Defense Prep

A defense is passed in the preparation. The committee's questions are predictable to anyone who reads the thesis the way a committee does: looking for the weakest link in each chapter, the claim that outruns the evidence, and whether the candidate understands the limits of their own work. This skill reads the thesis that way, generates the questions, and rehearses the answers.

## Step 1: Read as the committee reads

Take the full thesis (or the proposal) and produce a chapter map: for each chapter, the question, the design, the main finding with its number, the identification evidence, the stated limitations, and the link to the thesis-level question. Then mark, per chapter, the three weakest points: the claim least supported, the assumption least defended, the exhibit most likely to draw a question. These are where the defense will be fought.

Also profile the committee where the user can describe them: each member's field, methods, and known concerns. A labor economist, a methodologist, and an education scholar ask different questions of the same chapter; prepare for each.

## Step 2: Generate the question bank

Forty to sixty questions, organized:

1. **Thesis-level** (ten): why these chapters together, what is the thesis's single contribution, what would change in the field if every finding is right, what unifies the identification strategies, what is the one thing the thesis cannot answer and why.
2. **Per chapter** (eight to twelve each): from the identification-defense attack list for the chapter's design, the peer-review-simulator's field-expert concerns, the data section (sample construction, measurement, attrition), magnitude interpretation, and the link between the framework's mechanism and the result.
3. **Method fundamentals** (ten): the assumptions of the estimators used, stated from first principles; what the estimand is; why this estimator over the alternatives; how inference was done and why.
4. **Hostile and broad** (five to ten): "why should anyone care", "isn't this already known from [closest paper]", "what if your main assumption is wrong", "what would you do differently", "how does this generalize beyond Brazil / this population / this period".
5. **Procedural**: what revisions the candidate anticipates being required and how long they would take.

## Step 3: Rehearsal answers

For every question, a written answer in three parts, fitting in sixty seconds spoken:

- **The concession or the direct answer first.** "Yes, that is a limitation, and the direction of bias is downward, so the estimate is conservative." Committees respect a direct concession and distrust a deflection.
- **The evidence**: the exhibit, page, or number that supports the answer, so the candidate can point to it.
- **The boundary**: what the thesis does and does not claim on this point.

Answers are practiced aloud, timed, and revised until they are short. Long answers signal uncertainty.

## Step 4: The defense talk

Built with the conference-presentation-deck skill, sized to the program's rules (often 20 to 40 minutes): the thesis question and the answer first, then each chapter as finding, design, identification evidence, and result, then the contribution and the limitations stated by the candidate before the committee raises them. Appendix slides mapped to the question bank so any answer can be shown, not just spoken.

## Step 5: The day

- Opening: state the thesis's contribution in two sentences before the talk starts; the committee will remember the frame.
- During questions: listen to the whole question, restate it briefly if it is long, answer directly, stop. If the answer is unknown, say so and say how it could be found; never improvise a result.
- Disagreement: a committee member who is wrong on a fact is corrected politely with the evidence; one who disagrees on judgment is acknowledged and the thesis's position restated once. Do not argue past that.
- Note every required revision as it is said; read them back at the end to confirm the list.

## After the defense

Convert the committee's required revisions into a plan with the response-to-reviewers discipline: each required change, the disposition, the section affected, and the timeline the program allows.

## Quality bar

- The question bank covers every weak point the chapter map identified.
- Every answer leads with the direct answer or the concession and cites an exhibit.
- The talk states the contribution in the first minute and the limitations before the committee does.
- The candidate has practiced every answer aloud under sixty seconds.
