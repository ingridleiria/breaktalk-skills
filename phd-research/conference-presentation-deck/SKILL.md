# Conference Presentation Deck

A research talk is not the paper read aloud. Its job is to make a room of skeptical colleagues understand the question, believe the identification, and remember the number. Everything else is cut, and what remains is shown rather than told.

## Sizing

- 12 to 15 minutes (conference session): 10 to 12 slides.
- 20 to 25 minutes (workshop): 15 to 18 slides.
- 45 to 90 minutes (seminar, job talk): 25 to 40 slides, with a clear map and a discussant-proof appendix.
- Poster: one panel, the same logic compressed, read in three minutes.

One slide per minute is the ceiling. Fewer is better.

## Deck structure

1. **The finding, first.** Slide one or two states the question and the answer with the number: "Student financing raised graduation by 8 points and learning by 0.12 SD." The audience decides in the first minute whether to listen; give them the reason.
2. **Why it matters**: the problem in the world, one slide, one number with a source.
3. **What was known and what was not**: two or three closest papers, one slide, framed as what their designs could not do. Not a literature review.
4. **Setting and data**: the institution, the policy, the years, the units, the sample size, in a slide the audience can read in twenty seconds. A timeline of the policy where relevant.
5. **Design**: the source of variation in words, the estimating equation once, the identifying assumption in one sentence. One or two slides.
6. **Identification evidence**: the pre-trend plot, the first stage, or the density test, shown as a figure, given as much time as the main result. This is where economists in the room decide whether to believe anything that follows.
7. **Main result**: one figure or one table with the main column highlighted, the magnitude translated into interpretable units on the slide. Then heterogeneity or mechanism, one slide each if established.
8. **Robustness**: one slide summarizing what was varied and that the conclusion held; details in the appendix.
9. **What it means**: one slide, the implication sized to the evidence.
10. **Conclusion**: the finding restated with the number, and the contribution in one line. End on the finding; the questions follow.

Appendix slides: every robustness table, alternative specifications, data details, and the answers to the anticipated questions, each reachable by a slide number stated in the talk.

## Slide rules

- Titles are sentences stating the slide's point. Reading only the titles gives the whole talk.
- One idea per slide. When a slide has two, split it.
- Figures over tables; when a table is necessary, show the one column that matters and grey the rest.
- Figures follow the academic-figures-monochrome conventions, sized for a projector: larger fonts, thicker lines, no legend inside the plot.
- Equations appear once, with each term explained aloud, never a wall of notation.
- Text at 24 pt or larger; no paragraph on a slide; no more than five lines.
- Citations on slides as author and year only.
- Consistent template, restrained palette; a university brand if the occasion calls for it, otherwise white with one accent.

## The script and the questions

- **Talk script**: what will be said on each slide, in speaking sentences, timed. Practice against the clock; talks run long, never short.
- **Anticipated questions**: fifteen to twenty, from the identification-defense attack list and from the peer-review-simulator's referee concerns, each with a two-sentence answer and the appendix slide to point to. Concede what should be conceded in the first sentence of the answer; rooms respect that more than a defense.
- **The one-slide summary** for a discussant or a hallway conversation.

## Poster variant

Question and answer in the title. Left column: setting, data, design. Center: the identification figure and the main result figure, large. Right: magnitude, mechanism, implication. Under 300 words of text in total. QR code to the paper.

## Delivery

Build the file with the environment's presentation skill, applying the rules above. Deliver the deck, the appendix, the script, and the questions sheet together.

## Quality bar

- The number is on the first or second slide and on the last.
- The identification evidence gets a full slide and a full minute.
- The titles alone tell the story.
- The deck fits the slot with time for questions.
- The questions sheet covers every attack a methodologist would raise.
