# Weekly Review and Planning

Two jobs do not fit in one week by effort; they fit by deciding, every week, which hours belong to which and what will not happen. This is the review that makes that decision on paper, so that the week is chosen rather than absorbed.

## The review (end of week, 30 to 45 minutes)

Work through in writing, briefly:

1. **What got done**, per commitment, as outcomes. Not the hours spent; the things that now exist that did not on Monday.
2. **What did not get done that was planned**, and the honest reason: no time, wrong estimate, avoided, blocked by someone else, deprioritized deliberately. The reasons over several weeks are the pattern to fix.
3. **What arrived unplanned** and consumed time: the fires, the requests, the meetings. Estimate the hours; this is the load the plan must leave room for.
4. **Deadlines moved or approaching**, across all commitments, in one list with dates.
5. **Open loops**: promises made to others, decisions waiting on the user, messages unanswered, each with the person and the date.
6. **Energy and health**: one line, honestly, because the plan for next week depends on it. This is a note for the user, not a diagnosis; if the line has been bad for several weeks, the plan includes rest before it includes work.

## The plan (start of week, 20 minutes)

1. **The one thing per commitment.** For each serious commitment, the single outcome that would make the week a success. Two commitments, two outcomes. Write them at the top of the page.
2. **Deep work blocks.** Each outcome needs uninterrupted time; identify the blocks (two to three hours, at the hours the user thinks best) and put them on the calendar before anything else. Protect them as meetings with oneself; move them only for a real emergency, and if moved, reschedule the same day. A PhD dies in the gaps between meetings; the block is what prevents it.
3. **Fixed commitments** already on the calendar: meetings, teaching, calls across time zones. Check them against the blocks and resolve collisions now.
4. **The deadline sequence** for the next two to four weeks, both commitments merged, so that a thesis deadline and a board meeting in the same week are seen three weeks out, not the Sunday before.
5. **The shortlist.** Beyond the two outcomes, the five to eight tasks that must happen this week, each with the day it will be done. Everything else goes on a "not this week" list, explicitly, so it stops occupying attention.
6. **What will not get done.** Written down. A plan without this section is a wish list.
7. **The load buffer.** Leave roughly a fifth of working hours unplanned for the unplanned; the review told you how much arrives.
8. **The people.** Who needs to hear from the user this week (the supervisor, the CEO, a collaborator, family), and when.

## Rules that keep it honest

- Estimate in hours and compare with the hours available; if the shortlist exceeds the hours, cut the shortlist, not the sleep.
- The deep-work block for the commitment that has no external deadline (usually the thesis or the side project) is the one most at risk; schedule it first and earliest in the week.
- Batch shallow work (email, admin, scheduling) into named slots rather than letting it fill every gap.
- One planning tool, one list; the plan lives where the user will look on Tuesday.
- Review before planning; plans made without the review repeat last week's mistakes.

## Monthly and quarterly extension

Once a month, the same review at the level of the month: outcomes against the quarter's goals for each commitment, the deadlines for the next quarter merged, and a decision about what the next month's one thing per commitment is. Once a quarter, a check on whether the balance between commitments is the intended one, using the hours actually spent, not the hours intended.

## Deliverables

A one-page weekly plan (outcomes, blocks, shortlist, not-this-week, people), the review notes kept in a running document so patterns are visible over months, and calendar blocks created for the deep work when a calendar tool is connected.

## Quality bar

- Two outcomes at the top, one per commitment.
- Deep-work blocks on the calendar before anything else.
- A written "not this week" list.
- Hours planned are less than hours available.
- The review was done before the plan.
