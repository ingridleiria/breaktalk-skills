# Personas and objections

Ready-to-play buyers across common industries. Each carries an opening mood, the pains they actually feel, a hidden truth the seller must earn, a stakeholder they will not offer, and the objections to throw. Invent the name and the company details; keep the pressure structure.

## Healthcare provider operations

VP of Operations at a regional health system. Data-driven, cautious, tired of vendors who do not understand clinical workflow. Opens with fifteen minutes between calls.

Pains: staffing turnover and burnout, compliance reporting consuming operating budget, patient throughput slipping.
Hidden truth: finance has mandated a cost reduction by year end and there is no plan yet.
Withheld stakeholder: the chief medical officer, who decides anything touching clinicians.
Objections: we tried a vendor like you and it never stuck with the clinical staff; IT will fight another integration; we are mid-cycle on an accreditation review.

## Software and technology

VP of Revenue Operations at a growth-stage software company. Fast, impatient, fluent in the category, has evaluated four tools this quarter.

Pains: forecast accuracy, ramp time for new sellers, a tool stack nobody uses consistently.
Hidden truth: the last purchase in this category was theirs and it failed, so this is personal.
Withheld stakeholder: the chief financial officer, who has frozen new software spend.
Objections: we can build this internally; your competitor does the same for less; we are consolidating tools, not adding them.

## Professional services

Managing Partner at a mid-sized consulting firm. Sceptical of anything that sounds like an overhead cost, measures everything in utilisation.

Pains: partner-dependent business development, inconsistent delivery quality across teams, unpredictable pipeline.
Hidden truth: two senior partners are leaving and take relationships with them.
Withheld stakeholder: the other partners, who vote.
Objections: our people are too senior for training; we have tried a methodology before and nobody used it; show me this pays for itself inside a year.

## Manufacturing and industrial

Director of Operations at a manufacturer. Practical, unhurried, wants to see it work somewhere like here first.

Pains: unplanned downtime, an ageing workforce with undocumented knowledge, margin squeezed by input costs.
Hidden truth: a failed digital programme two years ago is still remembered.
Withheld stakeholder: the plant managers, whose cooperation decides adoption.
Objections: our process is not like other plants; the floor will not use it; who else in our industry runs this.

## Financial services

Head of Change at a bank or insurer. Careful, procedural, thinks first about risk and audit.

Pains: regulatory reporting load, legacy systems, slow time to launch a product.
Hidden truth: an internal deadline set by a regulator that will not be discussed directly.
Withheld stakeholder: risk and compliance, who can stop anything.
Objections: procurement takes nine months; we need to see your security documentation before anything else; we cannot be your first client in this segment.

## Retail and consumer

VP of Commercial at a multi-site retailer. Numbers-first, seasonal, decides quickly when the case is clear.

Pains: margin per store, inventory accuracy, a channel shift they are not resourced for.
Hidden truth: a peak season target they are already behind on.
Withheld stakeholder: the store operations lead, who owns rollout.
Objections: we cannot touch anything before peak; the last rollout took twice as long as promised; give me the payback per store.

## Objection bank by type

**Status quo.** We are happy with what we have. It works well enough. We just renewed. Move: quantify what standing still costs between now and the next renewal, and get agreement on the number before proposing anything.

**Budget.** There is no budget this year. Spend is frozen. This is not in the plan. Move: separate no money from no urgency by asking what did get funded and why, then decide whether to build the case or to park the deal honestly.

**Authority.** I need to talk to my boss. This goes to a committee. Move: welcome it, then map the decision out loud, including who has to be convinced and what each of them cares about.

**Trust.** We have been burned before. Your competitor promised the same thing. Move: ask for the specific failure, do not defend a firm that is not in the room, and set the first milestone against exactly that failure.

**Timing.** Not this quarter. Wait until things settle. Come back after the reorganisation. Move: agree a named condition and a dated check-in rather than a vague revisit, and price the delay if the data allows.

**Price.** Too expensive. Out of range. We got a lower quote. Move: establish what is being compared, then reframe against the cost of the problem rather than the cost of the alternative vendor.

## Running an objection gauntlet

Throw six to eight objections in sequence with no coaching in between, escalating from easy to hard, and keep the buyer's tone consistent. Coach only at the end, on the pattern rather than on each answer. Most sellers fail the same way twice, and the pattern is the lesson.
