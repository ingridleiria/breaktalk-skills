# Contractor MSA and Task Order

A firm that places people onto client engagements needs two papers, in a fixed order. The master services agreement is signed once with the individual and holds everything that does not change: confidentiality, intellectual property, non-solicitation, liability, termination, governing law. The task order is issued per engagement and holds everything that does change: role, client, dates, services, deliverables, and money. Issuing a task order without a signed master agreement is the mistake this skill exists to prevent, because it leaves the firm with commercial terms and no protective ones.

This skill drafts documents. It is not legal advice, and final execution goes through counsel.

## Two gates, confirmed before drafting

1. **Qualification.** The contractor meets whatever standard the firm requires for the role: certification, licence, background check, insurance, references. If the user has not confirmed it, ask. Do not draft for an unqualified person without flagging it in the draft.
2. **Signing order.** A task order is issued under an executed master agreement and references it by date. If no signed agreement exists, produce the master agreement first and state the order explicitly. When a request jumps straight to the statement of work, ask whether the agreement is signed, and if not, produce both in the same pass.

## Which document to build

| Request | Build |
| --- | --- |
| A new contractor joining the bench, a master agreement, a partnership agreement | Master agreement |
| Putting a contractor on a named project, a task order, an Exhibit A, an SOW for a person | Task order |
| A new contractor going onto their first project | Both, master agreement first |

## Intake

Take what the conversation already contains and ask only for what is missing. Never invent a commercial term. An unknown fee, date, or bonus percentage stays as a bracketed placeholder, visibly, so it cannot be signed by accident.

**For the master agreement:** full legal name of the contractor, their location, the entity name and signatory on the firm side, the effective date, and any standing terms that differ from the firm default.

**For the task order:** the task order number, the date of the master agreement it is issued under, the role title, the client or engagement name, the term with start and end dates and any split between full and part time, the services the contractor owns, the deliverables and milestones with dates, the payment schedule by month or milestone, any bonus or success participation, and the expenses policy.

Keep a blank intake template and one filled example beside the skill. The example is the reference for tone and depth, and is worth more than any instruction about it.

## Master agreement anatomy

Do not reorder. Title. Draft notice while under review. Preamble naming both parties. Recital stating the role and the qualification held. Then: term; services and the fact that specific work is ordered by task order; use of subcontractors; background checks; representations and warranties; change control; compensation and expenses; relationship of the parties, stating independent contractor status explicitly; cooperation; tax responsibility; confidentiality, split into definition, exclusions, and the non-disclosure obligation; inventions and assignment of intellectual property; return of documents on termination; non-solicitation with its duration; non-interference; indemnity; limitation of liability; assignment; termination; general provisions covering governing law, forum, dispute resolution, notices, severability, and entire agreement; acknowledgement; signature block.

## Task order anatomy

Title, naming it as Exhibit A and giving the task order number. Draft notice while under review. Incorporation preamble, referencing the master agreement by date and stating that its terms govern. Effective dates. Role. Description of services, as bullets a reader can test work against. Key deliverables and activities, with dates or milestone triggers. Compensation: a short introduction, then the schedule as a table, then the payment terms, including any back to back condition tying payment to the client paying the firm. Bonus or success participation, if any, with the trigger and the calculation. Expenses, with the approval rule and the invoicing deadline. Incorporation and priority, stating which document wins on conflict. Acknowledgement. Signature block.

## Standing terms

Every firm has terms that do not move from contract to contract: governing law and forum, dispute resolution, the non-solicitation period, the intellectual property position, payment timing, and the invoice submission deadline. Record them once in a `standing-terms.md` beside this skill and apply them consistently. When a request would change one of them, do not change it quietly. Flag it, name the clause, and say what the change exposes the firm to.

## Output

Produce the document as a formatted file suitable for signature, following the firm's document standard for typeface, heading colour, header, and footer. Keep the draft notice on every review copy and remove it only for the version going for signature. Validate the file opens cleanly before delivering. For a new contractor, deliver both documents together and state the signing order in the message, not only in the file.

## Quality bar

- The qualification gate and the signing order are confirmed in writing before drafting.
- No commercial term is invented; unknowns are visible brackets.
- Clause order follows the anatomy above, and the task order references the master agreement by date.
- Standing terms match the recorded set, and any deviation is flagged rather than absorbed.
- The draft notice is present on every review copy, and the file is validated before delivery.
