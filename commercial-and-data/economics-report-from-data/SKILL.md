# Economics Report From Data

Description is not analysis. "Revenue grew 25 percent" is description. "Revenue grew 25 percent because one channel is structurally replacing another, which means the business compounds rather than restarts each quarter" is analysis. This skill enforces the second standard, and refuses to ship the first.

## Workbook discipline

Any workbook produced alongside the narrative follows the same rules the report depends on: raw data present as its own sheet, every analysis cell a live formula reading from it, derived fields referencing computed cells rather than recomputing, a visible reconciliation cell that reads zero, genuine assumptions in labelled input cells, and a header note per tab documenting which columns it reads. A report whose figures cannot be traced to a cell is not deliverable.

## The four levels

Start at level one and climb to whatever the question demands. Every report reaches level three. Anything with an executive summary reaches level four.

1. **What happened.** The number, the period, the comparison, stated precisely and without interpretation.
2. **Why it happened.** The mechanism. Which specific change produced the number, named and dated.
3. **What it means.** The economic consequence: revenue quality, risk, scalability, unit economics, predictability.
4. **What to do.** The recommendation the analysis supports, with the cost of not doing it.

## The frameworks

**Channel economics.** When two or more revenue channels exist: the share of each, the rate the mix is shifting, which channel has the better economics, and the value of a one point shift. A mix shift is structural when it persists across periods, is driven by product or programme change rather than promotion, and is not reversing. Otherwise treat it as cyclical and say so.

**Concentration risk.** Top five above half of revenue is high concentration. A single entity above fifteen percent is a meaningful dependency, above twenty five percent a critical one. The question that matters is not the share but the anchoring: a large client held by a contract, an integration, or a programme is a different risk from a large client buying order by order. Express the exposure as replacement: losing this account requires replacing the equivalent of N average mid-tier accounts.

**Revenue predictability.** Split revenue into what arrives without a new selling motion and what requires one each time. Recurring revenue is worth more per unit because it capitalises at a multiple, costs nothing to re-acquire, allows operations to be planned, and is harder to lose. The conversion opportunity is therefore real even when total revenue does not change: the same dollar becomes a better dollar.

**Growth decomposition.** Total growth splits into more entities and more revenue per entity, and revenue per entity splits into frequency and average size. Naming the component tells you whether the trend is durable. An annualised run rate is only valid when the elapsed months are complete, no exceptional item distorts the average, and seasonality has been accounted for. Always state the assumption in the sentence that carries the number.

**Whitespace and conversion.** Where comparable entities show very different penetration of a valuable channel, the gap is the opportunity. Size it three ways, using the lowest, the average, and the highest penetration observed among comparables, and report all three. Presenting only the upside as "the opportunity" is the most common way an analysis loses its credibility.

**Cost of delay.** Delayed revenue is forgone, not deferred. An initiative that launches in September does not recover June, July, and August. Estimate the monthly revenue each delayed initiative would produce once live, using benchmarks from comparable initiatives already running, multiply by months lost, and report per initiative and in aggregate at conservative, base, and upside rates of the benchmark. Say plainly that there is no recovery mechanism.

**Seasonality.** With multiple cycles of data, separate the seasonal baseline from the directional trend before describing anything as growth or decline. Report which periods systematically outperform, the peak to trough ratio, and whether the pattern is stable across years. The implication is usually about timing: an initiative that misses the peak waits a full cycle for the next one.

## Insight quality tests

- **The five second test.** If the sentence would be true for any business in any industry without changing a word, it is a statement, not an insight.
- **The so what test.** After every figure, ask what follows. If the answer is "it is interesting", the insight is unfinished. An insight ends in a decision, a risk, or an action.
- **Traceability.** Every number points to a cell, a tab, or a calculation. Do not round in a direction that improves the story.
- **Falsifiability.** Say what would have to be true for the claim to be wrong, and what evidence would overturn it. A claim that cannot be wrong is not analysis.

## Report templates

**Executive summary, one page.** Headline finding as a sentence with a number. Three findings, each at level three or four. The single largest risk, sized. The single largest opportunity, sized three ways. The recommended action and the cost of delaying it.

**Entity deep dive.** Total revenue by period. Share through the recurring channel against the portfolio average. Trend. Economic profile in two sentences. Opportunity if penetration is low, expansion if it is high. Risk, stated as replacement cost.

**Cost of delay brief.** Number of delayed initiatives. Monthly forgone revenue at conservative, base, and upside. Cumulative at three, six, and twelve months. The principle stated once. The priority list with the reason each item ranks where it does.

**Channel shift memo.** Share moved from x to y over the period. The one to three specific changes that drove it. The value of a point of shift at current scale. The structural or cyclical verdict with its evidence. The trajectory and the next milestone that would confirm the thesis.

## Language standards

Replace "revenue went up" with the rate. Replace "a big opportunity" with the sized conversion and its assumption. Replace "this is a risk" with the exposure and the replacement requirement. Delete "seems to suggest", "might indicate", "significant", and "strong growth", and put the magnitude or the mechanism in their place. If three consecutive sentences carry no figure, the draft is still in description.

## When the data will not support the conclusion

Say so explicitly, in the report, not in a caveat at the end. The data shows X and does not show Y, which would require Z. This series is too short to separate structural from cyclical. This figure is directionally right but approximate because of a known issue in the source. Overstating certainty costs more than admitting a limit.

## Quality bar

- Every report reaches level three, every executive summary reaches level four.
- Every number is traceable and every assumption is stated in the sentence that uses it.
- Opportunities are sized three ways, never only at the upside.
- Structural and cyclical are distinguished explicitly, with the evidence given.
- The report ends in actions, each attached to the figure that justifies it.
